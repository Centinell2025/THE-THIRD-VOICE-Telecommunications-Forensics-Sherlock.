# THE THIRD VOICE

**Fictional Telecommunications Cybersecurity & DFIR Sherlock**

> **IMPORTANT — FICTIONAL CYBERSECURITY TRAINING ENVIRONMENT**
>
> THE THIRD VOICE is a **100% fictional cybersecurity and digital-forensics laboratory**. It does **not** involve real telephone interception, real surveillance, real people, real companies, real telephone numbers, real carrier systems, or access to real communications. All identities, calls, extensions, numbers, domains, IP addresses, CDRs, SIP/PBX records, authentication events, network artifacts, timestamps, and investigative events used by this Sherlock are synthetic, fictional, sanitized, or generated specifically for an isolated training environment.

## Purpose

This Sherlock trains a **cybersecurity forensic investigator** to examine already-acquired fictional telecommunications evidence. The participant's role is retrospective forensic analysis: validate evidence, preserve integrity, reconstruct events, correlate artifacts, distinguish normal technical failures from suspicious activity, document uncertainty, and issue an evidence-based conclusion.

The participant is **not** being trained to intercept communications.

## Scenario

Several fictional employees report unusual behavior during confidential corporate calls. A forensic investigator receives a fictional evidence package and must determine whether the anomalies are ordinary technical failures, unauthorized activity, or evidence consistent with a communications-security incident.

## Mission

Preserve the evidence, reconstruct the calls, correlate telecommunications and system artifacts, identify anomalies, respect investigative boundaries, and issue only conclusions the evidence can support.

## Forensic Boundary

Analyze **only the evidence supplied with this fictional case**.

This Sherlock does not authorize or require:
- live interception or recording of communications;
- monitoring of real calls;
- access to real carrier, PBX, voicemail, or telephone accounts;
- acquisition of third-party call records;
- credential or authentication bypass;
- scanning or probing external telecommunications systems;
- surveillance of real individuals;
- interaction with real telephone infrastructure.

**The investigator must stop where the evidence stops.**

## Core Forensic Rule

**A forensic conclusion must never exceed what the available evidence can prove.**

Echo, static, dropped calls, unusual audio, routing anomalies, or user suspicion alone are not proof of interception. Findings must be supported by the supplied forensic artifacts and clearly distinguish facts, supported inferences, and unknowns.

## Evidence Families

- Chain-of-custody and SHA-256 records
- Fictional Call Detail Records (CDRs)
- Synthetic SIP signaling logs
- Synthetic PBX audit and routing logs
- Sanitized/synthetic network-capture summaries
- Fictional authentication records
- Synthetic endpoint and administrative events
- Fictional time-source and clock-offset records
- Fictional case notes and witness statements

## Investigation Phases

1. Evidence Preservation
2. Telecommunications Reconstruction
3. Anomaly Correlation
4. Attribution Boundaries
5. Forensic Conclusion

## Final Determination

The analyst must classify the allegation as **SUPPORTED**, **NOT SUPPORTED**, or **INCONCLUSIVE**, and cite the supplied fictional evidence supporting that determination.

No command recipes are provided to participants. Tool selection and investigative method are part of the forensic challenge.
